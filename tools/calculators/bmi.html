How to Use

About This Tool

The BMI (Body Mass Index) Calculator assesses whether your weight is in a healthy range relative to your height. BMI is a screening metric used by healthcare professionals worldwide to identify potential weight-related health risks. The calculator supports both metric (kg/cm) and imperial (lb/ft-in) units and provides category classifications based on WHO and CDC guidelines.

Input Guide

Enter your height and weight in your preferred unit system. For metric, use kilograms for weight and centimeters for height. For imperial, use pounds for weight and feet/inches for height. The tool automatically converts between systems. Optionally enter age and gender for more tailored health insights, though BMI categories are primarily based on height and weight alone.

Output

The calculated BMI value is displayed with one decimal place. The tool classifies your result into standard categories: Underweight (<18.5), Normal weight (18.5–24.9), Overweight (25–29.9), and Obese (30+). A visual gauge shows where you fall within the healthy range. The tool also estimates your healthy weight range based on your height.

Tips & Notes

  • BMI does not distinguish between muscle and fat — athletes may have high BMI despite low body fat.
  • Children and teens require BMI-for-age percentiles rather than adult BMI categories.
  • Waist circumference and body fat percentage provide additional health risk context beyond BMI alone.
  • Consult a healthcare provider for personalized medical advice rather than relying solely on BMI.

Common Use Cases

Individuals track fitness progress during diet and exercise programs. Nutritionists screen clients during initial consultations. Insurance companies use BMI as one factor in health risk assessments. Fitness trainers set realistic weight goals for clients. Researchers collect population-level obesity statistics. Travelers verify luggage weight limits against personal metrics.
